The Knight of Pentacles is the slowest of the four knights, and the one who goes all the way to the end. While the Knight of Wands gallops flat out, the Knight of Cups glides along unhurried on his swan and the Knight of Swords charges like a storm, the Knight of Pentacles keeps his black horse at a standstill and gazes quietly at the single coin in his hand. He looks stopped, but that is in fact this knight's method. In terms of court-card maturity, the Knight is the stage of finishing the Page's learning and going out into the world to carry things out — and what makes the Knight of Pentacles distinctive is that he interprets carrying things out as something never to be rushed.

## Symbols and the number

Of the four knights, only the Knight of Pentacles is drawn with his horse standing still. That is not incidental staging but a device that shows the nature of the earth element exactly as it is. Wands (fire) stands for speed, Cups (water) for flow, Swords (air) for the judgment of a moment; Pentacles (earth) stands for being packed down and for lasting. In the court hierarchy a Knight originally means action, charge, speed — but meeting the earth element turns that drive from an explosive gallop into dogged persistence. The field has been turned over as well, hinting that this knight's next destination is not a battlefield but a place of labor.

## Upright — the one who finishes

Upright, the Knight of Pentacles is someone you can rely on without any flash at all. Work handed to him gets finished, and a date promised gets kept. In this season it works in your favor to accept that finishing matters more than speed. There is no need to rush even when other people look as though they are pulling ahead.

## Reversed — diligence that stalled

Reversed, the Knight of Pentacles is the state where being reliable has gone too far and turned into stubbornness and standstill. Knowing the method is outdated and not changing it; holding to the familiar road even at the moment change is called for. If it comes up reversed, rather than doubting the diligence itself, start by checking whether the direction you are pouring it into is still the right one.

## Readings by situation

- **Career** — Upright earns the trust of your manager and colleagues through a dogged attitude and carries the project you were given all the way to the end. Reversed is the state of insisting on an old way of working until the trend leaves you behind, so the obstinacy needs to be set down for a while.
- **Love** — Upright is a season where a consistent attitude gives the other person a sense of security. Reversed, being overly careful can make the progress of the relationship feel frustrating, so add some life to it now and then with something unexpected.
- **Money** — Upright has you filling your monthly savings target steadily, month after month. Reversed, being too conservative can let a decent opportunity pass by untouched, so keep the safety but open your ears a little more to information.
- **Study & Exams** — Upright sees a long stretch of exam preparation through to the finish with unwavering persistence. Reversed carries the risk of moving so slowly that you never cover the full syllabus before the test, so some of the perfectionism has to go.
- **Health** — Upright, keeping a set routine faithfully every day builds up your stamina. Reversed, insisting on the same routine alone makes it easy to lose flexibility in the body, so try a different kind of movement occasionally.

## The one piece of advice this card offers

Do not measure your pace against anyone else's. What the Knight of Pentacles proves is one thing only: that if you are slow and do not stop, you arrive in the end. Just leave yourself the room to halt on the road now and then and open the map again, so that dogged persistence never hardens into stubbornness that stops checking its direction.
