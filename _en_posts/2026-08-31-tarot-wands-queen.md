Some people walk into a gathering and become its center without trying, and their confidence brightens everyone standing nearby. When a reading is asked who that person is, the card that turns up is very often the Queen of Wands.

> The Queen of Wands leads the people around her through confidence and charm. Upright holds a room with warm charisma. Reversed is that same energy tipped over into jealousy or display.

## Reading it by suit and number

In the Rider-Waite deck the Queen of Wands sits on a throne decorated with lions and sunflowers, a staff in one hand and a sunflower in the other. A black cat keeps her company at her feet. The lions stand for courage and self-possession, the sunflower for an open nature that turns its face toward the sun without hesitation. The black cat symbolizes the ability to handle intuition and shadow alongside that bright exterior.

Read court cards as stages of maturity in a person and the Queen is the seat where the Page's pure curiosity and the Knight's headlong drive have ripened inward into ease. At this stage the fire element of the Wands suit stops blazing and becomes a quieter heat that does not go out. The room to light up the people around her without burning through anything in a hurry — that is the maturity the Queen of Wands shows.

Upright, the Queen of Wands shows the strength to lead through confidence that was built rather than charm that was handed over. She knows her own worth well enough to feel no need to measure herself against anyone, and that ease is exactly what puts the people around her at ease too. Reversed appears when the confidence loses its direction and runs toward jealousy or display. The urge to prove herself takes the lead, moods start swinging, and it can come across as an attempt to control the people nearby. Once the sunflower wilts, only the cat's shadow is left.

## Readings by situation

**Career** — Upright brings out leadership that energizes a team through confidence and warmth. Reversed can push a leader into snapping at teammates on a mood swing or drifting into unilateral decisions, so the ease to consider where your colleagues stand is needed.

**Business** — Upright means charismatic management and personal warmth building a following around the brand. Reversed is closer to a warning that spontaneous decisions driven by emotion can leave the organization confused.

**Love** — Upright points to a period of leading the relationship with a self-assured, warm attitude. Reversed is where jealousy takes the lead, or an attitude that steers the relationship too much ends up creating distance instead.

**Relationships** — Upright shows you becoming the center of a gathering through approachable, self-assured charm. Reversed can draw resentment through a quiet urge to control other people, so keeping your generosity is the key.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the Queen of Wands always refer to a woman?**
A. No. The gendered imagery of court cards is a traditional symbol only. In practice the card points to an attitude or an energy — inwardly ripened confidence — that anyone can carry regardless of gender.

**Q. Does reversed mean I should dial my confidence down?**
A. It is not asking you to reduce the confidence itself. Reading it as a signal to check whether that confidence is pointed toward comparison and display is more accurate.
