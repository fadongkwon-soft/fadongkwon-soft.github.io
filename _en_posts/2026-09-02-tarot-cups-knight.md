Among the sixteen court cards the Knight always stands between the Page and the Queen — finished with learning, not yet arrived at full maturity. Of the four Knights, the Knight of Cups is the seat where the element of water runs over most romantically, and he wears the face of a young man who has begun to move on feeling.

## Reading the symbols

- **The white horse walking calmly** — unlike the galloping horse of the Knight of Wands, this one keeps a measured pace, led by feeling and still holding its control.
- **The winged helmet and boots** — ornaments that stand for imagination and ideals, revealing that this figure is chasing a romantic vision.
- **The cup held out in front** — a gesture of offering, of handing something over. It stays at the stage of a proposal, with no way yet to know whether the other side will accept.
- **The path following the river** — feeling is what leads this journey, and at the same time a hint that where the road ends is as fluid as the current.

Upright, the Knight of Cups means a period when a warm, romantic offer is on its way to you. Courtesy comes through in the tone and the manner, and consideration for the other person leads the way. Reversed points to that warmth stopping at words with nothing behind them. The feeling may well have been sincere, but with no action following, the promise stays an unrealistic one.

## Readings by situation

The place the Knight of Cups turns up most often is, unsurprisingly, a scene where feelings and relationships are being exchanged.

- **Love** — Upright is a period when warm, romantic goodwill knocks at your heart. Reversed can shake your trust with sweet words that cannot be kept, or with an indecisive attitude.
- **Career** — Upright opens the door to an appealing job offer or an invitation to collaborate. Reversed calls for going through the terms of the contract carefully so that a proposal with vague conditions does not carry you away.

The same pattern repeats in relationships that carry a little more weight, like a business partnership or a marriage. Upright, an attractive offer or a thoughtful gesture genuinely becomes the thing that moves the relationship forward; reversed, it takes the caution of giving it time and watching whether the offer stays at the level of talk. Questions about investment deserve particular care — the sweeter the return being promised, the more it matters to check the concrete basis and the verifiable facts first.

## Combinations that change the reading

The Knight of Cups is a card whose impression shifts a great deal with the cards around it. Alongside **the Two of Cups** it shows an offer with the potential to grow into a serious partnership; alongside **cards from the Swords suit** it reads as a signal that those romantic words have to pass a rational check. Paired with **cards from the Pentacles suit**, on the other hand, the question becomes how far the offer matches actual practical conditions. On its own the Knight of Cups tells you only that an offer is coming. Whether it is sincerity or good manners is answered by the cards beside it and by the actions that follow.
