How does someone sit on a throne rocking in the middle of a swelling sea and still wear a face that calm? **The King of Cups** is the card that holds the answer.

> **The King of Cups in one line** — the card of a mature person who neither suppresses feeling nor gets swept away by it, but handles it with real skill.

## Where the meaning comes from

Unlike the other three kings, the King of Cups is drawn on a throne that seems to float on a heaving sea rather than to rest on solid ground. Behind him a ship sails through a storm; off to the other side a single fish leaps clear of the water. The ship is reason moving forward without wavering in the middle of emotional turbulence, and the fish is the unconscious and the intuition, still very much alive and in motion. He holds a scepter in one hand and a cup in the other, both lightly, which says that authority and feeling coexist here without colliding. In the court cards the king is the seat of outward completion, authority and responsibility, and once the suit of emotion is set into that seat the portrait finishes as "someone who governs feeling well enough to turn that maturity into social responsibility." It is the same maturity of water the Queen carries, but where hers turns inward, his is exercised outward.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the King of Cups means emotional control, generosity and maturity. He keeps his composure even in a crisis, with room enough to hold the feelings of the people around him as well. If this card comes up, it is a time to lead the situation calmly while acknowledging what you feel, rather than being pushed around by it.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the King of Cups points to feeling pushed down and a coolness kept up only on the surface. Giving nothing away can leave the people around you frustrated, or held at a distance. Here it helps to let a little out — to one person you trust, if not more — rather than hiding it completely.

## Readings by situation

**Career.** Upright is a period of leading a team gently and maturely, without being shaken by feeling. A crisis is exactly where this steady kind of leadership shows its worth. Reversed is a curt manner that reveals nothing, leaving the team frustrated, so it is worth saying what you think a little more openly.

**Marriage.** Upright is a period of holding the center of the household steady with a generous, grown-up attitude. The relationship carries on with a spouse supported by a wide capacity to accept. Reversed is a current where a spouse easily feels distance because nothing gets expressed, so it is better to let the coolness go and offer a warm word first.

**Love.** Upright is a period that adds stability to the relationship through mature, generous acceptance. You have enough room to take the other person's ups and downs in stride. Reversed can open a distance because you hide what you feel and act cool about it, so expressing it honestly serves the relationship better than holding it down.

**Business.** Upright is a period of keeping your composure through a crisis and settling the organization with experience. A dependable manner turns directly into trust from outside. Reversed makes it easy to miss an opportunity by meeting shifting conditions with indifference, so listen a little more closely to the voices on the ground.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. How is the King of Cups different from the Queen of Cups?**
A. Both stand for an emotionally mature person, but the direction differs. The Queen's maturity works inward, as empathy and care; the King's works outward, as responsibility and leadership. Read them as two expressions growing from the same root.

**Q. Does a reversed King of Cups mean the person is cold?**
A. There is no need to conclude that. The card says less about someone's underlying character than about a current in which feeling is not being shown right now. Let the situation change and the heart can open again easily enough.
