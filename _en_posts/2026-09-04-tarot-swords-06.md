A boat pulls away with rough water behind it — is this a picture of running away, or of moving forward? A ferryman works the pole, a hunched woman and a child sit at the bow, and six swords stand upright and neatly arranged inside the hull. The Six of Swords quietly answers that question.

> The Six of Swords says that even if you have not fully escaped the hard stretch, you have at least started rowing in that direction. The fact that you moved at all is already evidence of recovery.

## Where the meaning comes from

The water ahead of the bow is smooth, while the water the boat has come from is still painted rough. That contrast is the heart of the Six of Swords. The storm has not vanished, but the movement is at least in the direction away from it.

The six swords standing tidily in the boat are worth a look too. This card does not show someone abandoning the problems and thoughts they came through — it shows them packed properly and carried along. That is also why the number 6 reads as the seat of harmony and recovery in the Swords suit. The conflict that broke into the open in the fifth position finally gets sorted and moved in the sixth. And the ferryman stands in for the person or the circumstance that helps from the side when you have no strength left to row on your own.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Six of Swords means moving out of a difficult phase and into something steadier. You have not arrived anywhere entirely new, but the direction is unmistakably toward better. Sometimes that is a literal change of place, a move or a new job; just as often it is a shift in attitude, or in the angle you look at the situation from.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the Six of Swords shows someone who knows they need to leave and still cannot lift their feet. Attachment to the past or an old wound holds the ankle, the next phase never begins, and the storm that has already passed is where they keep living. It looks like a person with the bags fully packed who cannot get into the boat.

## Readings by situation

### Property & Housing

Upright is a move out of hard living conditions — noise, a poor environment — into somewhere steadier. Even if the new place is not perfect, the odds are good that it is clearly better than the last. Reversed points to hesitating over a move again and again out of attachment or anxiety about the old place, so it helps to judge by the conditions in front of you now rather than the memories behind you.

### Career

Upright is a period of leaving a team or a project full of friction and moving into a relatively stable environment. Settling into the new seat takes time, but the direction is clearly improving. Reversed is hesitating over a new challenge because of a failure or a bad experience at a former job, so treat the old experience as reference only and try fitting yourself to the rules of the new place.

### Love

Upright is a difficult stretch of conflict giving way to a calmer, steadier relationship. It helps to focus on the relationship from here rather than chewing over past arguments. Reversed is carrying old hurt around because you cannot shake it off, so you need to give yourself the time to finish processing that feeling properly.

### Health

Upright is coming out of a wearing stretch of fatigue and easing back onto the road to recovery. Keeping a calm environment, without pushing yourself, is what helps your energy come back. Reversed points to putting off exercise or activity because of the memory of an old illness or injury, so it is better to start again with a light walk.

### Money

Upright is moving out of a difficult spending structure and gradually into a steadier flow. A consistent saving habit will firm this up more than any dramatic change. Reversed is being held back by the memory of past financial trouble and never fully enjoying the stability you now have, so it helps to accept the changed situation as it actually is.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the Six of Swords mean I will actually move house or change jobs?**
It can, but it does not necessarily mean physical movement. A shift in circumstances or in perspective alone is enough for this card to appear. What it actually means depends on the context of the question and the cards that come with it.

**Q. If it is reversed, does that mean I can never get out?**
It is more accurate to read it as a signal that you are not quite ready to leave, or that some attachment remains, rather than that the way is blocked. Naming what is holding your ankle is the first step into the next phase.
