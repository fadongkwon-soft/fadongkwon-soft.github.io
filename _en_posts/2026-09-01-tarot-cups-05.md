"I can't stop thinking about the person I broke up with." "Someone I trusted let me down." If the **Five of Cups** turns up on a question like that, the card does not deny the loss you are feeling. What it says is that beyond that feeling something is still there which has not come into view yet.

> A figure wrapped in a black cloak looks down at three fallen cups, but two cups are still standing upright just behind them. It means something remains in the place a grieving gaze does not reach.

## Reading it by suit and number

Cups are the water element, governing feeling and relationships, and across the Minor Arcana the number 5 stands for conflict and lack. Where the two meet, the Five of Cups becomes the card of emotional loss, regret and the ache of expectations that did not line up. The river in the picture and the castle beyond the bridge hint that a place to return to, or a way forward, still exists even if it looks far off right now. The black cloak means being deep under grief, and it matters just as much that the figure has merely turned their back rather than being shut in completely. Where the boredom of the Four of Cups was feeling sitting still, the Five of Cups is the more active grief that arrives after something has been lost.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Five of Cups means a mind pulled toward loss and regret. The trouble is that the gaze stays on the three spilled cups — on what has already happened. More than the loss itself, it is the inability to look away from it that makes this period harder.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed is the point where you rise out of that grief. It is the stage of finally noticing the two cups standing behind you, the things that remain, and starting to walk again. Less that the wound disappears, and closer to the process of accepting that you can go forward while still carrying it.

## Readings by situation

Asked about love, upright means a period when it is easy to be taken over by a slight you felt or a regret already past. Looking at the warmth still beside you helps recovery more than clinging to what is gone. Reversed has you back on your feet, gathering yourself and repairing the relationship, and room opens up to tend the old wound and reach out again.

In family relationships, upright shows a period when it is easy to stay tangled in past regrets or old resentments, and reversed shows those wounds being tended, with forgiveness and reconciliation coming into reach.

Asked about marriage, upright is a period when it is easy to fix only on where your partner fell short of what you expected, while reversed shakes off a long-held resentment and moves toward peace. Looking at the person who is beside you now rather than staying with what has passed is the advice this card repeats.

In wider relationships, upright means a mind taken over by disappointment or a slight felt from a friend, and reversed means shaking that feeling off and beginning to make peace.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the Five of Cups always mean a breakup or bad news?**
No. The Five of Cups is closer to a card about the direction your heart takes toward a loss than about the loss itself. It can mean an actual parting, but just as often it indicates nothing more than a state of resentment or regret.

**Q. Does a reversal guarantee recovery?**
It is more accurate to read it as a sign that your heart is ready to head toward recovery than as a guarantee of it. For the relationship or the situation to actually improve, the action has to follow — turning your own head toward the two cups still standing behind you, toward what is left.
