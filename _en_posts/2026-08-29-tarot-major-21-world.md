The World sits at the place where the twenty-two card journey that set out from 0, the Fool, finally comes all the way around and closes. Carrying the last number of the Major Arcana as it does, this card turning up reads as a sign that one story — large or small — has entered its closing stretch.

## Upright vs reversed at a glance

| Upright | Reversed |
| --- | --- |
| completion, achievement, one cycle closing | unfinished, a delayed ending |
| standing at the point you aimed for | almost there, with the last knot still to tie |
| time to celebrate and sketch the next journey | time to drop your guard and recheck the details |

## Symbols and the number

At the center of the World card in the Rider-Waite deck, a dancer draped in violet cloth dances inside an oval wreath woven from laurel leaves. An oval has neither a beginning nor an end, which makes it a shape that is finished and at the same time still holds room for the next turn of the cycle. At the four corners of the wreath sit a human (or an angel), an eagle, a bull and a lion — the same four beings that appeared on the Wheel of Fortune, commonly read as the constellations Aquarius, Scorpio, Taurus and Leo, as the four elements of earth, water, fire and air, and further as the four suits of the tarot's Minor Arcana (Pentacles, Cups, Wands, Swords). The World, in other words, is the picture where four different elements finally come into harmony inside a single circle. As a number, 21 is the last in the Major Arcana, and adding 2 and 1 gives 3, so it carries the cyclical sense that a completion is itself the seed of the next act of creation.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the World is the moment something you have worked at actually reaches completion. Earning the qualification you were after, finishing a project you prepared for a long time, tying off one period and stepping into the next. Like the dancer inside the wreath, this is a moment where it is fine to enjoy the achievement in full.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the World points to a state where the finish line is right there and the last step keeps getting later. The overall direction is not wrong; more often it is some minor piece of finishing or a small detail that has you by the ankle. The fact that you have made it nine tenths of the way does not change, so calmly checking over what is left is the faster route than fretting about it.

## Readings by situation

- **Money** — Upright has the lump sum you were aiming for finally coming together, or a large financial goal getting met. Reversed is a state where you relax in front of a target that is nearly full and let spending creep up, so it is better to hold the tension in your saving right to the end.
- **Career** — Upright is a period where a long project wraps up cleanly and marks a high point in your career. Reversed can mean a small detail slipping through at the finishing stage and pushing completion back, so the final review deserves real attention.
- **Love** — Upright is a period where you feel deep satisfaction in each other and the relationship bears one of its fruits. Reversed can leave an unfilled regret behind even when things look calm on the surface, so it is better to work through it in an honest conversation than to paper over it.
- **Study & Exams** — Upright brings a long exam process to a successful end that comes back as graduation or a pass. Reversed can lead to a disappointing result from a thin final review, so keep going through the core material right to the end.
- **Property & Home** — Upright is a period where a home purchase or a moving plan you prepared for a long time comes together. Reversed can mean a minor defect or a finishing problem pushing the schedule back at the last stage before you move in, so a careful inspection beforehand is worth the trouble.

## The one piece of advice this card offers

The advice the World card gives across every situation is this: the closer you are to the end, the less lightly you should treat the last step. Walk ninety-nine steps and stop on the hundredth, and the completion is simply postponed. Take that one step instead and, like the dancer inside the wreath, the whole process up to here becomes fully your own.
