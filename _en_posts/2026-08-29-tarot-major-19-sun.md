## Where the meaning comes from

The Sun in the Rider-Waite deck is built to pair with the card right before it, 18, the Moon. If the Moon draws the unease of night, a truth half concealed, shapes blurred into shadow, the Sun is the morning that arrives after it — the clarity you only get once the fog has lifted. The great sun with a face drawn into it stands for an honesty that hides nothing and shows everything, and the naked child on a white horse, without armor or saddle, stands for a plain joy that covers up and dresses up nothing. It also means there is no room left for fear to slip in. The sunflowers craning over the wall behind are every one of them turned toward the sun, showing that even with a wall in the way the light ends up reaching past it. As for the number, 19 adds to 10 and from there back to 1, so it also reads as a bright cycle beginning again after one knot has been tied.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Sun is a card of achievement and vitality that needs no second-guessing. It points to a stretch where results show themselves without any packaging, where situations resolve clearly without long explanations. When this card comes up there is no reason to work at hiding your confidence, because it is a sign that you are standing exactly where the effort you have built up gets its share of sunlight.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed does not put the sun out. It points instead to the state where light bounces back and dazzles you — where you get so taken with your own results that you stop seeing what is around you. It is the moment confidence slides quietly into overconfidence, and bright energy into carelessness. Just as it takes the sunflowers time to climb over the wall, the reversed Sun is "a brief patch of shade," not "the light going out." What is worth noting, though, is that the cause of that shade is usually not something outside you but your own inattention.

## Readings by situation

**Love** — Upright is a stretch where neither of you has to hide how you feel. The more you express it the brighter the relationship gets, so there is no reason to hesitate over an honest show of affection. Reversed is the moment to be careful about taking that brightness for granted. Rather than leading with a small point of pride, better to hand the person who stays beside you a word of thanks.

**Career** — Upright has the results you have built up becoming visible, with the people around you acknowledging them. Sharing the pleasure of the achievement with the colleagues who worked for it, rather than keeping it to yourself, is what makes the sunlight last longer. Reversed is a time when it is easy to get drunk on results and claim other people's share as your own, so it calls for a step back and an honest word of gratitude.

**Money** — Upright opens some room to breathe through an unexpected bonus or a rise in income. The real advice the Sun offers, though, is not to spend that abundance down to the last of it, but to leave part of it for what comes next. Reversed is a state where money that arrives suddenly lifts your mood and loosens your spending, so the more your earnings grow, the more it pays to tighten the saving habit.

**Health** — Upright is a stretch where your condition climbs to its best, and with energy to spare it is a good time to move your body. Reversed calls for caution about trusting that energy so far that you overdo it. Precisely when you feel in good shape, keeping the rhythm of water and rest is the secret to making it last.

**Study & Exams** — Upright is the point where the ability you have built shows up in the exam room exactly as it is. Go in with confidence and put down everything you know. Reversed makes it easy to relax over a small win and get sloppy about the final check, so the better things are going, the more you need to keep hold of that last review.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is the Sun always a good card?**
It is fair to say it is the card in the Major Arcana that reads positively with the least baggage attached. Rather than "always," though, it is more accurate to call it the card where what you have already built comes to fruit. It does not mean luck falling out of the sky with no effort behind it; it means the process you have been through finally coming into the light.

**Q. If the Sun comes up reversed, does something bad happen?**
A reversal does not announce misfortune. The strength of the light is unchanged; the signal is that some carelessness has crept into how you handle it. Rather than a serious worry, it is enough to take it as a light caution — that precisely while things are going well, it is worth covering the basics one more time.
