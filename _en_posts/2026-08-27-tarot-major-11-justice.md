Every deck maker put Justice in a slightly different seat. In the older French Marseille tradition, Justice sat at 8 and Strength at 11, but in the Rider-Waite deck most widely used today the two swapped places, leaving **Justice at 11 and Strength at 8**. The reordering is said to have been made so that position 11 would line up with Libra, the scales, in the astrological correspondence table — and the fact that a single card's number has a backstory like that suits Justice rather well, since it was always the card about clean, exact standards.

## Reading the symbols

- **The sword in the right hand** — held straight up, the sword stands for judgment that divides clearly, with no blur. The key detail is that it stands vertical, tilted toward neither side.
- **The scales in the left hand** — two pans in equilibrium mean the balance of cause and effect, of what is given and what is taken. Weight is measured by facts and evidence, not by feeling.
- **The seat between two pillars** — Justice always sits between the two pillars, dead center. It marks a neutral position that takes nobody's side.
- **The angular composition and restrained palette** — elements that give form to holding to principle without emotional wavering.
- **The eyes looking straight ahead** — a gaze that faces facts alone, with private feeling set aside.

### Upright — an outcome that follows the rules

Upright, Justice means effort and result lining up honestly. It shows its power especially in places with explicit standards, like contracts, evaluations and negotiations. This is a period when evidence and documentation decide the outcome rather than emotional appeals, so staying objective works in your favor.

### Reversed — when the scales tip

Reversed points to standards tilted to one side, or to not receiving the share you are owed. Loopholes in a contract, an unfair evaluation, someone dodging responsibility all belong here. Rather than responding emotionally to the unfairness, it is more effective to organize your supporting evidence and calmly ask for it to be corrected.

## Readings by situation

Starting with the quick keyword sweep by situation.

- **Business** — a period when fair contracts and transparent dealing turn into results / watch for breach-of-contract or unfair-dealing risk
- **Career** — an evaluation season where performance and reward line up justly / meet an unfair evaluation with documentation rather than emotion
- **Real estate & housing** — a contract is only safe if you check the registry and the rights attached carefully / neglect it and the risk lands on your deposit
- **Money** — income and spending balancing transparently in the ledger / fix receipt and tax errors immediately to stop the loss
- **Love** — a period for fairly negotiating what each of you wants / empathy comes before assigning blame

Two of these, the contract-related ones, are worth unpacking a little further. Justice shows up unusually often in **business and real estate dealings**. In both areas, a single line of a document or one clause carries a lot of weight. Upright can be read as a sign that the terms you have been reviewing are being settled reasonably; reversed means it is time to look again for a special provision or an ownership issue you may have missed.

**Money and workplace evaluation** work the same way. This card reacts sharply to the question of whether you are getting as much as you should. Upright, this is a period when past effort comes back as fair compensation, so there is no need to shrink at the negotiating table; reversed, preparing performance records and evidence first is far more persuasive than voicing your grievance emotionally.

## Combinations that change the reading

When **the Wheel of Fortune** appears next to Justice, the reading narrows from "chance good luck" to "exactly what your effort earned, returned to you." When **the Tower** appears with it instead, the phase may be one where accumulated unfairness or distortion can no longer hold and comes out all at once. When **the Lovers** appears alongside it, a choice within a relationship is being put to the test, and you can read it as a period that demands a decision leaning toward neither side.
