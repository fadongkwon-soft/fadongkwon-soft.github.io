You thought you were nearly there, so why do the shoulders feel heavier than ever? The Ten of Wands is the card that asks about the moment you are carrying the largest load of all with the finish line already in sight.

> The Ten of Wands walks toward a goal that is almost reached, hauling every last burden along with it. Upright it signals that the going is hard but the finish is not far off. Reversed it signals that the load needs to be shared, or partly set down.

## Where the meaning comes from

In the Rider-Waite deck, the Ten of Wands shows a figure walking with ten staves gathered up in both arms. The bundle blocks the view ahead, and still the steps do not stop, aimed at a village that sits small in the distance. What is worth noticing is that nobody threw these staves at the figure — they were picked up voluntarily. Where the staves of the Seven or the Nine of Wands were tools for fending off an outside threat, the staves of the Ten are responsibilities and promises shouldered by one's own hands in order to reach a goal.

The number 10 is the seat where a cycle completes, and at the same time the point where things have stacked up so far that they tip into excess. The fire element of the Wands suit — will and drive — arrives here to find that the original enthusiasm has turned into weight to be carried. The paradox at the heart of this card is that the load does not lighten as the goal gets closer; instead every responsibility accumulated up to the final moment has to be carried at once.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Ten of Wands shows that the destination is not far, however hard the going is. The weight on the shoulders right now is the sum of everything achieved and taken on, and the village coming into view means there is a place to set it all down before long. What does deserve attention is that the walking is being done with the view blocked. This is also a period where it is easy to miss the other signals around you because the goal has swallowed all of your attention.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed appears when the weight of the load has crossed the line of what can be handled. The habit of shouldering every responsibility alone is now the very thing standing in the way of finishing. The message the card offers here is not to give up but to share the load or set part of it down. There is no need to carry all ten staves to the end. Put a few down, hand a few to someone else, and the destination is still reachable.

## Readings by situation

**Career** — Upright is a steady push toward the finish in the middle of an overwhelming workload. Reversed is a state pressed right up to the edge of burnout, so delegating part of the work to a colleague or a junior is the better move.

**Family** — Upright is a period of faithfully bearing responsibility for the family and holding on. Reversed can end in exhaustion from trying to shoulder every burden alone, so the roles need to be divided among the family members.

**Marriage** — Upright is a process of getting through it together, however heavy the housework, the childcare and the financial pressure. Reversed points to being worn out on your own because the situation was never shared honestly with your spouse.

**Money** — Upright has you carrying debt or spending pressure conscientiously and whittling it down bit by bit. Reversed is a signal to step out of an obsession with saving and let some air back in.

**Relationships** — Upright is a period of taking on plenty of requests and roles and building trust in the process. Reversed means it is time to clear out the relational burdens you took on because you could not say no.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Is the Ten of Wands always a bad card?**
A. No. Upright is closer to a sign that the goal has come within reach. What the card adds is that the road there is not a light one.

**Q. If it comes up reversed, should I quit what I am doing?**
A. Not necessarily. Reading it as a signal to set part of the load down or share it is more accurate. Simply sorting out your priorities can reduce the weight.
