How do you drive a black sphinx and a white sphinx in the same direction when neither one is bridled? The answer Major Arcana 7, **the Chariot**, offers is unexpectedly plain. Not with physical force, but with a settled will and a gaze that does not waver.

> The Chariot is a card of will that pulls two opposing forces under a single goal and pushes forward without hesitation. Upright it means strong drive and victory; reversed it means a runaway with no direction, or motion that goes nowhere.

## Where the meaning comes from

The black sphinx and the white sphinx that pull the chariot are not tied to any reins. That tells you the charioteer leads two opposing forces — feeling and reason, impulse and restraint — in one direction using nothing but pure will and concentration. The canopy over the chariot is scattered with stars, hinting that this victory reaches past the quarrel in front of him and touches a larger order. The crescent moons on his shoulders connect back to the moon symbolism of the High Priestess, standing for a balance that neither suppresses the swings of emotion nor gets dragged along by them. The walls and city behind him show the scene of leaving one already settled world and moving into new territory. The number 7 is the next step after the choice made in the Lovers (6) — the place where resolve gets pushed into real action, and therefore tested.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Chariot means **drive that carries you toward a goal without flinching**. Even with opposing conditions or obstacles in the way, a clear direction lets you convert those very forces into forward motion. What distinguishes the victory this card describes is that it comes from a definite goal and unbroken focus, not from luck.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the Chariot shows that same drive **with its direction lost**. When the two sphinxes pull toward different places, the chariot only rocks in place. Push ahead on enthusiasm alone with no plan and you tend to burn through your energy and increase the friction around you instead. What a reversal calls for is not slowing down, but checking again whether the direction was right to begin with.

## Readings by situation

**Business** — Upright is a stretch where strong execution puts you ahead of the competition. If you have a clear goal in place, now is the time to push boldly. Reversed can mean failing to regulate your pace and overextending, so it is safer to review your cash flow and your realistic constraints together.

**Career** — Upright is a period of visible results and noticeable growth. Real confidence lets you work through what is in front of you at high density. Reversed can leave you out of step with the people around you from too much eagerness, or simply busy without direction, so it is worth stopping to review your priorities.

**Love** — Upright means moving toward the other person without hesitating when you feel something, and the relationship advances. Confidence gets better results here than hedging. Reversed can mean leading with your own pace and failing to consider the other person, so leave yourself room to match their stride.

**Investing** — Upright means trusting the plan you set and holding to it without wobbling. The grit not to react to every small piece of market noise is what pays off. Reversed can mean excessive conviction or leverage that leads to sloppy risk management, so slow down and check your safeguards first.

**Study & Exams** — Upright is explosive concentration that produces a visible jump in scores over a short period. The clearer your goal, the better your efficiency. Reversed makes it easy to skip reviewing wrong answers because eagerness runs ahead, so you need the balance of putting accuracy before speed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. The Chariot and Strength both look like cards about mastering something. How do they differ?**
A. The Chariot leads an external subject — the sphinxes, the situation — forward with direction and will, while Strength gently soothes and masters the inner impulse symbolized by the lion. If the Chariot is drive aimed outward, Strength is closer to patience aimed inward.

**Q. If the Chariot appears, does that mean I have to hurry?**
A. The heart of this card is not speed itself but whether the direction is clear. If you are certain about the direction, going fast is fine, but hurrying without one leads to the runaway the reversal describes. Setting the goal clearly comes first.
