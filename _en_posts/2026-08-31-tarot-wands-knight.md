The Knight of Wands is already spurring the horse forward before the thought has finished forming — the card with the most sheer speed in the whole Wands suit.

## Upright vs reversed at a glance

| Field | Upright | Reversed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core meaning | charging ahead, adventure, fast action | haste, fickleness |
| Attitude | headlong drive | impulsive spontaneity |
| Advice | turn the passion into action | check once more before acting |

## Symbols and the number

In the Rider-Waite deck the Knight of Wands is drawn with the staff raised high above a horse rearing on its hind legs. The tunic embroidered with salamanders carries the same fire symbol as the Page's, but this time it is no longer something to be looked at — it is worn, and it has become the force that carries him forward. The plume on the helmet streaks in the wind, and the horse already has its forelegs driving ahead. There is not a single still element anywhere in this card.

Read court cards as stages of maturity in a person and the Knight is the seat right after the Page. The curiosity that watched and learned as the Page has now been put into action. The Knight is the point where the fire element of the Wands suit — will and drive — shows itself in its rawest form. Few cards keep the distance between thinking and doing as short as this one.

Upright, the Knight of Wands shows the adventurous spirit and drive to jump in without hesitating. It is the force that moves first instead of calculating when an opening appears, the force that breathes new life into a situation that had stalled. Reversed appears when that speed loses its direction. Pushing ahead without a plan creates friction, or the enthusiasm fails to last and moves somewhere else almost immediately — plain fickleness. The horse still runs well; where it is running needs another look.

## Readings by situation

- **Career** — Upright has strong drive breathing life into a stalled project. Reversed can turn a rushed report or an impulsive move into friction at work.
- **Business** — Upright is a period of breaking into new accounts through aggressive selling and action on the ground. Reversed can create problems in the follow-up after pushing ahead without a careful plan.
- **Love** — Upright wins the other person over with unreserved passion. Reversed can leave the other person startled by an approach that came too fast, or by swings of mood.
- **Investing** — Upright is a period of reading the market quickly and responding nimbly. Reversed carries the risk of enlarging losses through repeated impatient trades, so setting your rules comes first.
- **Study & Exams** — Upright is a period of conquering a difficult unit in one go on explosive concentration. Reversed can mean leaning on last-minute cramming and missing the parts that actually mattered.

## The one piece of advice this card offers

What the Knight of Wands advises is not to stop but to check the direction once, then run. The drive in this card is never the weakness. What is worth remembering is simply that it is hard to look behind you while you are on horseback. Sketch the destination briefly before setting off, and the speed particular to the Knight of Wands becomes the strongest weapon you have.
