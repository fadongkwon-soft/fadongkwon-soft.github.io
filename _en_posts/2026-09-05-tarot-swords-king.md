The King of Swords is no longer a card about someone swinging a blade. It draws someone holding the blade upright to keep a principle. Seated in the last chair of the Swords court, this figure looks straight ahead and holds the throne without a flicker of movement, and that stillness is exactly the finished authority the card describes.

## Upright vs reversed at a glance

| Field | Upright | Reversed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core keywords | rational judgment, principle, authority | dogmatism, no flexibility |
| Basis for a decision | fair principles and evidence | a conviction of his own |
| Way of communicating | clear, consistent explanation | one-way instruction |
| What is needed | backing the authority with evidence | allowing for exceptions and circumstances |

## Symbols and the number

- **Facing straight forward** — the only figure in the Swords court looking ahead rather than to the side. It stands for meeting a situation head-on instead of stepping around it.
- **The upright sword, both feet planted** — judgment that does not waver and principle with roots. A center of gravity that feeling cannot pull off balance is the heart of this card.
- **The butterflies on the throne** — the same butterfly motif carved into the Queen of Swords' throne. A hint that the King's composure is not innate coldness either, but something compacted out of trial and error already lived through.
- **Clear sky and a bird** — a long view taken from somewhere above the turbulence of emotion.
- **The last seat in the court** — the place reached after the Page's watching, the Knight's action and the Queen's inner ripening. The King of Swords is the stage of turning the principle earned through all of it outward and answering for it.

**Upright**, the card says this is a time to speak from evidence rather than feeling. Setting a fair standard and holding to it consistently is what builds trust. **Reversed** is the state where that principle has lost its give and set into stubbornness. The shadow of the card shows when he treats only his own judgment as correct and stops accounting for the other person's circumstances. A reversal does not mean abandoning the principle — it signals that the way the principle gets applied needs enough flexibility to allow an exception.

## Readings by situation

- **Career** — Upright is a period of leading the organization's important decisions with deep knowledge and firm principles. Reversed can draw pushback through an inflexible attitude, so the circumstances of the people involved need weighing too.
- **Business** — Upright is a period of controlling risk systematically and making steady management calls. Reversed can smother the organization's creativity through too much authority, which is worth watching for.
- **Family** — Upright means the role of the dependable adult who guides the household's important matters with wisdom and fairness. Reversed can make the family shrink back under an attitude that is too rigid, so an open mind is needed.
- **Relationships** — Upright is a period of becoming the trusted presence who settles conflict cleanly through fair judgment. Reversed can open distance with the people around you by pressing only your own yardstick.
- **Study & Exams** — Upright is a period of working through difficult material confidently on a solid base of knowledge. Reversed makes it easy to be thrown by a new type of question after insisting on the familiar method, so a flexible approach helps.

## The one piece of advice this card offers

The authority the King of Swords holds does not come from raising his voice. It comes from consistent principles, the evidence that backs them, and the discernment to know when to apply them flexibly. Spend less effort on proving you are right and more on delivering that rightness in a way the other person can accept, and you come a good deal closer to the authority this card draws.
