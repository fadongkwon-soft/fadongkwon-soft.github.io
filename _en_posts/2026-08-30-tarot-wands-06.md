The Six of Wands holds the moment when sweat you shed alone finally gets confirmed by somebody else's applause.

## Upright vs reversed at a glance

| Field | Upright | Reversed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Core meaning | victory, recognition, good news | going unrecognized, watch for arrogance |
| Emotional state | confidence, a sense of achievement | feeling slighted, or self-satisfied |
| Advice | enjoy the result openly | stay humble and build real substance |

## Symbols and the number

In the Rider-Waite deck's Six of Wands, a figure wearing a laurel wreath rides past on a white horse. Another wreath hangs from the tip of the staff in hand, and people carrying staffs walk alongside, welcoming the procession. The laurel has marked victory since antiquity, and the white horse stands for the conviction that moves forward without fear. What matters is that this victory does not belong to one person alone. The fact that the people around him are walking with him is what makes this the card of recognized victory.

The number 6 is the place of harmony and recovery that arrives after the competition and disorder shown by card 5. The Wands suit belongs to the element of fire and governs passion, will and the drive to act, so the Six of Wands can be called the point where effort pushed hard at last turns into a balanced harvest.

Upright, the Six of Wands shows effort bearing fruit and being recognized by the people around you. Visible results follow — passing an exam, a promotion, a project that lands — and the core of this card is that other people also see those results. Still, this card is a way station rather than the end of the journey. Take in the moment of applause fully, but be ready to pick up the next staff as well.

Reversed, two shadows can appear at the same time: the recognition or reward you expected gets delayed, or the opposite, a small result goes to your head. The first is the case of feeling slighted because the value of your effort does not show right away; the second is the case of overtrusting a victory already won and missing the next opportunity. Recall that the figure in the laurel wreath has not dismounted, and the reversed warning reads as one message — do not stop just because the result tasted good.

## Readings by situation

- **Career** — Upright has your results officially recognized, leading to a promotion or a commendation. Reversed points to morale sagging as the expected reward runs late, or the opposite, self-satisfaction blocking the next result.
- **Business** — Upright is a time of pulling ahead of the competition and drawing attention in the market. Reversed warns of the risk of settling into short-term success and handing your place to a later entrant.
- **Love** — Upright is a period of winning someone's regard and gaining confidence in the relationship. Reversed is a signal that an attitude of showing yourself off can come across as a burden to the other person.
- **Study & Exams** — Upright is a time when your grades match the effort and recognition follows from the people around you. Reversed is a caution that letting your guard down after one good result can leave you shaky on the next exam.
- **Relationships** — Upright widens your circle amid support and congratulations. Reversed is a warning that frequent self-praise can quietly earn resentment.

## The one piece of advice this card offers

The advice the Six of Wands gives is simple. Do not hide the result — show it, and share it with the people who made it. Like the people walking beside the figure in the laurel wreath, a victory that looks won alone usually has somebody's help hidden inside it. Keep your humility in the moment of applause and this card's victory becomes the stepping stone to the next one.
