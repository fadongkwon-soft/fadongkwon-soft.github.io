One of the most common misreadings when you first learn tarot is assuming that a court card always points to an actual person. Sometimes it does, but the Page of Wands is closer to a card about the youngest curiosity inside us — the attitude of wanting to try anything, clumsy hands and all.

## A closer look at the picture

- **A young figure standing in the middle of a desert** — a position not yet settled anywhere, on the verge of setting out to explore.
- **The salamander-patterned tunic** — the salamander was believed to live inside fire, and it stands for the fire element that runs through the whole Wands suit.
- **The gaze fixed on the staff, as if it were a curiosity** — pure interest in a new tool whose handling has not been mastered yet.
- **The pyramids in the background** — unfamiliar, foreign ground, territory nobody has walked yet.
- **The feathered cap** — a light, free attitude, closer to spontaneity than to formality.

## Upright meaning

Court cards are commonly read as stages of maturity in a person. The Page sits at the very front of that line, a stage with no real skill yet and no shortage of willingness to learn. Upright, the Page of Wands shows a new interest or a piece of welcome news arriving. The fire element particular to the Wands suit has only just caught here, so the heat is not large yet, but it is unmistakably there. If this card turns up, following plain curiosity is better than weighing the outcome. Even a clumsy attempt counts as learning on its own.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed appears when that curiosity loses its focus and scatters in every direction. The pull toward new things is the same, but nothing gets pursued to the end — everything just gets touched briefly. Since the position of the Page already assumes inexperience, reversed is where that inexperience surfaces as mistakes and distraction. What is needed then is not to suppress the curiosity but to practice staying with one thing a little longer.

## Readings by situation

### 💼 Career

Upright is a period of learning new work with a beginner's mind and laying down the fundamentals. Not being afraid to ask questions is what leaves the better impression. Reversed has you touching too many tasks until your focus scatters and mistakes come more often, so it helps to practice settling into one job at a time.

### 💕 Love

Upright is a period where pure curiosity and welcome news blow fresh air through a relationship. A casual message or being the first to say hello can turn into a good opening. Reversed can invite misunderstanding through an attitude that looks short on seriousness, so even inside the lightness the sincerity toward the other person needs to come across clearly.

### 📚 Study & Exams

Upright is a good stretch for sinking into the pleasure of learning itself. Not hesitating to ask about what you do not know is what turns into ability. Reversed makes it easy to let time slip away on distractions because focus has scattered, so starting by tidying up your study environment is the better move.

### 💡 Business

Upright is a period where information about a new trend or product comes in and the seed of a business forms. It is still the exploration stage, though, so studying and validating come before any hasty investment. Reversed warns of the risk of pouring resources into something that has not been tested.

## What being a beginner really means

Read court cards as stages of maturity and the Page can look like the lowest seat of all. But in the Wands suit, what the Page lacks — the Knight's charge, the Queen's ease, the King's authority — is traded for a purer motive than any of them. Being clumsy at the start of something is not embarrassing; it is the natural place everyone passes through. The message the Page of Wands offers comes down to this. It is fine not to be good at it yet, so go ahead and follow the curiosity.
