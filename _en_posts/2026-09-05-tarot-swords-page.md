On a hill where the wind is driving hard, a figure with a still-young face grips a sword upright in both hands and glances over one shoulder. Hair and clothing whip in every direction, and behind, clouds and flocks of birds scatter untidily across the sky. Placed in the youngest seat of the Swords court, **the Page of Swords** draws exactly this — a sharpness not yet fully learned.

## Reading the symbols

- **The wind-lashed hill** — the background where the air element shows itself most openly. It stands for a lively mind that cannot sit still for a moment.
- **The sword held upright in both hands** — he has not fully learned how to swing it yet. The power to handle truth is there; the skill to modulate that power precisely belongs to the next stage.
- **The glance over the shoulder** — a posture holding curiosity and wariness at once. Keenly responsive to new information, and just as easily shaken by a small stimulus.
- **Scattered clouds and flocks of birds** — news and information flying in from every side. The Page's role is to gather it; this is not yet the seat where the final judgment gets made.
- **Position as a court card** — the Page is the first stage of the Swords court. If the Knight stands for action, the Queen for inner ripening and the King for outward authority, the Page is the origin point that comes before all of them, watching and learning.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Page of Swords is the card of **sharp observation and gathering information**. A period marked by the wish to grasp a situation as it actually is rather than reach a conclusion about it. Being the youngest seat in the court, it is also a card that values the learning posture itself instead of demanding finished judgment. If it comes up upright, read it as a time for asking questions and collecting material rather than settling on a confident conclusion.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the Page of Swords is the same sharpness jumping out untrained. **Hasty conclusions, certainty about unverified information, words with an edge that land wrong** are the typical forms. The shadow of the card shows when he forgets he is still in the learning stage and speaks or acts as though he already knows everything. If the card is reversed, it is time to check once more whether what you hold is genuinely confirmed fact.

## Readings by situation

### 💼 Career
- **Upright** — a period of collecting and analyzing industry trends and internal information quickly. Good for preparing a thorough report out of the material you gather.
- **Reversed** — a time when passing on unverified information carelessly draws gossip easily. Saying less until the facts are confirmed is the safer route.

### 💕 Love
- **Upright** — a period of watching the other person's reactions carefully and exploring slowly. Rather than deciding fast, observe with plain curiosity.
- **Reversed** — a time when a suspicious eye or a sharp tone can wear the other person out. Setting a little of the wariness down helps the relationship.

### 📚 Study & Exams
- **Upright** — a period of picking up and organizing the latest exam trends and information keenly. Material collected carefully becomes the base for a strategy.
- **Reversed** — a time when unverified rumors can shake you off course in your studies. Trusting only officially confirmed material is the safer path.

### 🤝 Relationships
- **Upright** — a period of observing the situations of people around you closely and gathering information. Better to leave it at observing, though, and not go testing people to see how they respond.
- **Reversed** — a time when groundless suspicion can grow into misunderstanding or discord. Instead of wariness, try an honest conversation first.

## If you drew this card today

Today is a good day to fill with observation and questions rather than conclusions. Look up something you have been wondering about, or sort through the material you have been putting off. If the card is reversed, though, repeating a story that has not been confirmed can bring misunderstanding down on you, so for today at least it is worth building the habit of checking once more before you pass on what you heard.
