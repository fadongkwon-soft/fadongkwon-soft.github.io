Most people's faces tighten when this card turns up. **Card 13, Death** — the skeleton knight, the card that shows up in every ominous scene in films and dramas. Among tarot readers, though, Death is known as the most misunderstood card in the deck. What it actually talks about is not dying but **an ending, and the new current that starts in the space it leaves behind**.

## A closer look at the picture

In the traditional Rider-Waite deck, a skeleton knight in black armor rides forward on a white horse. It looks like a frightening scene, but there are reversals hidden in every corner.

- **The banner in the knight's hand** carries a white rose. It is a promise of clean renewal after the end.
- **The sun is rising on the horizon.** Not a setting sun but one climbing between two towers — the other name for an ending is a beginning.
- **The knight does not stop; he advances.** Kings and children are equal in front of him. The current of change goes around no one.

Despite the superstition about the number 13, in the journey of the Major Arcana card 13 comes after card 12, the Hanged Man, the card of pausing and shifting your view. Once you have sat with something long enough, the order says, it is time to tie it off and move on.

## Upright — endings are where beginnings start

Upright, Death is the card of **a major transition**. It is the strength to cleanly tie off an old habit, a relationship that is over, a method that has run out of life. What matters here is that the card is much closer to "emptying" than to "losing." Just as a room has to be cleared before new furniture comes in, Death means the tidying that makes room for a new current.

If the card comes up upright, there is one question to ask yourself. **What am I still holding on to?** Habit, relationship, work — whatever it is, the next chapter opens the moment you clearly end what has already ended.

## Reversed — a hand that cannot let go

Reversed, Death is the state of **refusing change** even while knowing a transition is needed. Attachment to a connection that is finished, insistence on a method whose time is up, the inertia of "but it is familiar." The most common situation when reversed Death appears is that you already know the answer in your heart. The card is only pressing you to find the courage to act on it.

## Readings by situation

### 💕 Love
Upright is the energy to clear out stale feelings or a pattern that keeps missing the mark, and start again. Shake off the old regret and make room for a new heart. Reversed is being tied to a finished connection or an old wound and unable to move forward — letting go of the attachment comes first.

### 💼 Career & Business
Upright is fully closing out a running project or an outdated way of working and shifting direction. In business, it is the time to cut the loss-making side and take off again in a new direction. Reversed is feeling the need to leave or change yet being unable to release the attachment — a warning that the longer you postpone the clean-up, the more it costs.

### 💰 Money & Investing
Upright is a fresh start where wasteful habits and a bad spending structure get settled. Begin with old debts and the cards you never use. Reversed is clinging to a losing choice and refusing to close it out, so recovery starts with admitting the decision you made.

### 🌿 Health & Habits
Upright is the time to cut off a stale habit that harms your body and come back new. Late-night eating, irregular sleep — you already know what needs to end. Reversed points to being stuck in chronic fatigue because the bad habit will not go.

## Why Death is not a bad card

People say there are no bad cards in tarot, and that is especially true of Death. The card showing up means **change is already underway**, and that you still have the chance to choose which direction it takes. What is frightening is not the card but the ending itself — and the current keeps moving while you postpone that ending. Death is the card that tells you to ride the current, that if the change is coming anyway, you may as well tie the knot with your own hands.
