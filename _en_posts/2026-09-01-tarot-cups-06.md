Follow the numbered cards of the Cups suit in order and a journey of feeling appears. Emotion freshly filled at the Ace reaches another person at the Two, spreads into joy shared by several at the Three, sinks into boredom at the Four, and goes through the ache of loss at the Five. The **Six of Cups** sits at the moment of recovery that comes after that ache, the place where the heart warms again through the memories you miss.

## Reading the symbols

In the traditional image, inside an old garden, a child hands a cup filled with white flowers to a smaller child.

- **The six cups filled with flowers** — what passes here is not emotion but a pure heart itself. Where the Two of Cups was an equal connection between a man and a woman, the Six is the uncalculating sharing of childhood.
- **The garden as a setting** — a snug space enclosed by a wall, standing for a past that felt safe, or a memory you keep protecting inside.
- **The figure walking away in the background** — many versions show a small figure at one side leaving the garden. It carries the double meaning that memory is something to hold on to and something to let go of at the same time.
- **What the number 6 means** — in the Minor Arcana, 6 stands for harmony and recovery. The figure who stood grieving over spilled cups at the Five reaches the Six and finds balance again by remembering the good times that have gone.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Six of Cups is the card that soothes the heart through fond memory and the feeling of an innocent time. An old friend who got in touch after years, a place you loved as a child, an old object that brings back why you started — these are what settle the mind now. Good memories from the past turn into strength for living in the present.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, that longing goes too far and tips toward **staying in the past or dressing the memory up**. Like a picture of someone who never leaves the walled garden, you cannot live in the present moment and keep comparing it to a time already gone. It is a state where you need to watch yourself, since the better a memory was, the more easily it gets romanticized.

## Readings by situation

- In family relationships, upright means a period of sharing childhood memories and confirming a plain, honest affection. Looking through old photos together and warming that bond again is one good way to do it. Reversed can spoil the present relationship by keeping you tied to old family problems, so what is needed is an attitude that looks at the family you have today rather than at the memory.
- Asked about love, upright is a period of sharing old memories or reviving how it felt at the start, while reversed shuts you inside a past memory, unable to see the present relationship whole.

Questions about property or where to live suit this card well too. Upright points to a move back to the neighborhood you grew up in or to a familiar area, where a place you are attached to puts you at ease. Reversed can bring disappointment at the aging infrastructure of the old neighborhood, so rather than deciding on memory alone it is better to check today's practical conveniences as well.

In married life, upright deepens affection as you go back over the fresh early memories of dating, while reversed lets comparisons between the good old days and the present grow easily into discontent.

## Combinations that change the reading

When the Five of Cups comes up beside the Six, it means you are leaning on memory while the ache of loss has not fully closed, and it reads as a sign that recovery is under way. Alongside a Major card of hope such as the Sun or the Star, the reading that good memories of the past really do carry into present hope grows stronger. Overlapping instead with a card of strong change like the Tower or Death, the weight shifts toward the reading that this is the time to put the memory in order and move forward rather than stay in the longing.
