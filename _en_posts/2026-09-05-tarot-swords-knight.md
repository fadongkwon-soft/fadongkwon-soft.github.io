If your body is already out the door before the thinking is finished, could the card you just drew be the Knight of Swords? The fastest-moving card in the whole Swords court draws **the moment a decision becomes an action** through a knight charging at full speed with his sword pointed forward.

> The Knight of Swords is a card of speed — a decision carried into action the instant it is made. Whether that speed comes out of a clear goal or out of a moment's excitement is what separates upright from reversed.

## Where the meaning comes from

The Knight of Swords in the Rider-Waite deck stands in sharp contrast to the other court cards. The Page stands on a hill scanning his surroundings, the Queen sits on her throne and reflects, the King holds his seat looking straight ahead — the Knight is the only one riding at a full gallop. The horse's mane and the plume on the knight's helmet stream hard backward, and even the dark clouds and scattering birds in the background are drawn as though they cannot keep up with the speed.

In the growth sequence of the court cards, the Knight comes right after the Page. Where the Page was the stage of observing information and learning from it, the Knight is the stage of taking that learning into the field. He has not yet picked up the seasoned control of pace that the Queen or the King has, though. So this card carries the virtue of drive and the shadow of haste at the same time.

## Upright meaning

Upright, the Knight of Swords means the force of charging at a clear goal with nothing held back. A period of getting a step ahead of everyone else through decisions made without dithering and fast execution. Like the posture with the sword out front, this card is strongest when the direction is unmistakable. Worth remembering alongside that, though: the more speed you pick up, the less room is left for looking around.

## Reversed meaning

Reversed, the Knight of Swords is closer to that same speed running wild with the direction lost. Feeling gets ahead of the goal, and hasty words or decisions make friction with the people around you easy. Here the advice the card offers is not "stop" so much as "slow down and check the direction again."

## Readings by situation

**Career** — Upright is a period of pushing work toward a goal fast and boldly and getting results out of it. Reversed is a time when hurrying too much makes friction with colleagues or skipped procedures likely, so teamwork deserves a little more attention than speed.

**Love** — Upright means the force of going straight where your heart points and moving the relationship forward. Reversed can startle the other person or weigh on them through a hasty tone or manner, so leave yourself room to watch their pace.

**Business** — Upright stands for the drive to spot a gap in the market quickly and jump in boldly. Reversed is a time when an impulsive decision can shake the organization internally, so stability deserves as much thought as speed.

**Relationships** — Upright is stepping up without hesitation when a problem appears and clearing it away cleanly. Reversed can leave the other person embarrassed by blunt phrasing, so I would dial the intensity of the delivery down a notch.

## Frequently asked questions

**Q. Does the Knight of Swords always mean acting rashly?**
No. Upright it means quick, bold execution toward a clear goal. It is the reversal that reads as haste.

**Q. How do the Page and the Knight of Swords differ?**
Where the Page is the stage of observing information and learning from it, the Knight is the stage of moving straight into action on the basis of it. The biggest difference is that this seat is marked by speed of execution rather than caution.
